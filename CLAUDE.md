# CLAUDE.md

A personal French course that the user studies every day. It is a static web app with no build step (`index.html`, `js/`, `css/`). The course lives in `data/` and the generated audio in `audio/`. README.md explains the data files, the markup in French texts, the tools and the roadmap, so read it first. This file adds how a unit is built and checked, and the story bible.

## Machines and commands

The course was built on Linux (`~/Data/Courses/French`) and moved to Windows in October 2026 (`C:\Users\muath\Documents\GitHub\French`, remote github.com/MuathHamidi/French). Run these from the project folder:

| | Linux | Windows |
|---|---|---|
| Run the app | `python3 tools/serve.py` | `python tools/serve.py` |
| Check the content | `python3 tools/validate.py` | `python tools/validate.py` |
| Check the app | `node tools/check_app.mjs [--homographs] [lesson ids]` | same |
| Make audio | `.venv/bin/python tools/make_audio.py [--prune]` | `.venv\Scripts\python tools\make_audio.py [--prune]` |

`.venv/` and `tools/voices/` are not in git, so each machine sets them up once (README › Pronunciation). On Windows, a throwaway script piped into `python -` reads and prints in the ANSI code page: set `PYTHONUTF8=1` for those (the tools themselves don't need it).

The scripts in `tools/build/` help build a unit; each one explains its use at the top. Run them from the project folder:

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `where.py`, `gap.py` | where a word is in the course; which words of a list are taught, helpers or missing |
| `frequency.py` | the course against the Lexique frequency list (README › Roadmap): the frequent words it lacks |
| `expand_forms.py` | writes a regular verb's forms from a placeholder such as `"forms": ["@er:arros"]` |
| `lexsync.py` | puts a unit's lessons in `curriculum.json`, removes promoted helpers and adds new ones |
| `homolist.py`, `phrases.py` | the links of some words, or every phrase match, in a `--save-links` snapshot |
| `precheck.py`, `say.py` | how the voice reads the new words or any text (run them with the venv's python) |

## Building a unit

From Unit 17 on, a unit has four 25-word vocabulary lessons (two on its topic, two on high-frequency words) and two or three grammar or sound lessons, about 100 new words in all. Its plan is the `planned` list in `data/curriculum.json`. In Part 3 (Units 41 and up), the two high-frequency lessons teach common words the course still lacks ("Common verbs (1)", "Common nouns (1)"…): frequent words that are neither taught nor helpers, and frequent helpers (`frequency.py --missing 200 --helpers 200`), leaving out the grammar words (*être, de, pour*) and the most basic helpers (*chose, homme, fois*). The two other lessons are advanced (C1) grammar.

1. Before changing anything, save a snapshot of every word link: `node tools/check_app.mjs --save-links links-before.json`.
2. Write the lessons, then replace the unit's `planned` list with its `lessons` ids (`lexsync.py`).
3. Reuse what exists: look in `data/lexicon.json` and earlier lessons first, and search a reflexive verb by its infinitive too (*se permettre* was already taught, though *je me permets* turned up nothing), and look up a compound noun as a whole: *maillot de bain* was taught, though *maillot* turned up nothing. Search a reflexive verb with *se* too: *se déguiser* (u23) had no bare forms until Unit 43. When a lesson teaches a helper word, move its entry into the lesson with the same id. Ids drop accents, so check that a shared id is really the same word: *presse* (the press) is not the helper *pressé* (in a hurry), so the new word gets its own id (*presse-n*). Add helpers for any other new word the texts need.
4. When a unit introduces a tense, add its forms to the `forms` of every verb (done so far for the imparfait, the future, the conditional and the present subjunctive, about 330 verbs; the subjunctive only needed the forms that differ from the present and the imparfait: *finisse, prenne, soit, fasse…*). Past participles and their agreements were completed in Unit 29 for the past conditional; a participle spelled like an existing word (*perdu, ouvert, assis*, or *levé* for *se lever* next to *lever*) was left to that word. Present participles followed in Unit 30 for the gérondif, on the same rule: *étudiant, suivant, courant, vivant, devant, pendant* belong to their other word, so a gérondif of those verbs needs markup: *en [étudiant|etudier]*. The futur antérieur (Unit 32) needed no new forms: it is the future of *avoir* or *être* + the participle. The passé simple came in Unit 33: all six persons for one-word verbs, and the third persons only for phrase verbs and idioms (*fit confiance*, *eut l'air*). A form spelled like another word was left to that word, so mark it up when it is the passé simple: *vit* and *vis* belong to *vivre* (*il [vit|voir] le monstre*), *but, bus, vins, durent, plus* and *tapas* to other words, and *plut* to *pleuvoir*. Make sure no new form collides with another entry. Derive new tenses from forms that exist (the conditional came from each verb's future forms) and listen-check one form per verb against the old one: the voice swallows the vowel of **-ouer** verbs (*jouerais*), which needs `FIXES`.
5. `validate.py` must print OK. When two taught words share a spelling, read the "Ambiguous" list it prints: the tie goes to the older lesson.
6. Review homographs with `node tools/check_app.mjs --homographs <new lesson ids>`, and fix wrong picks with `[word|id]`. That list only shows single words with several entries, so also read every phrase match in the new texts (`phrases.py` over a `--save-links` snapshot): a phrase can take a word in another sense, like *ne quitte pas* (keep your eyes on) read as *ne quittez pas* (hold the line).
7. Generate the audio with `make_audio.py --prune` and read its IPA report for the new words (`precheck.py` gives the same report before the audio is made). The report always lists some harmless variants (e/ɛ, o/ɔ, ɥ/y, a schwa kept or dropped). When a word really sounds wrong (often English loanwords like *scotch*, *stress*, *liker* or *streaming* (*spoiler* is read like an *-er* verb), foreign names like *Saint-Exupéry*, *Copenhague* or *Danemark* (read in English, like *maghrébin*), abbreviations (the voice reads *BD* as *boulevard*, *SAV* as a word, and *CAP* alone as *cap*: `CASE_FIXES`), Roman numerals (*Louis XIV* came out as *Louis quatorze romain*), some passé simple forms (*vécut* is read in English), slang and spoken words (*thune* is read in English, *cimer* like an *-er* verb, *ben* and *nan* as written), *plus* meaning *more*, whose s the voice drops: *il en faut deux fois plus*, *plus que tout*, *on écoute plus qu'on ne joue* (but not *ne… plus que*, only), *-ille* read as in *ville*: *cheville*, *compagnie* (the voice says /kɔ̃pani/), *flamenco* (read with a nasal), *flashs* (the voice says the s), *punch, toast* and *DJ* (read in English), *fournil* (the l is silent), the adjective *négligent* (read like the verb *ils négligent*), *corner* (read like an *-er* verb), the plural noun *supporters* (read like the verb *supporter*), *body* (read in English), the numbers *quatre-vingt-deux* to *quatre-vingt-neuf* and *quatre-vingt-onze* (the voice says the t of *vingt*), or a liaison the voice adds (*au cas où, le cas échéant*) or loses after a fixed word (fix *des émojis* as a phrase)), add it to `FIXES` and run again; only the changed texts are regenerated.
8. `node tools/check_app.mjs --compare-links links-before.json` must print OK. Read its warnings: every older text that now links a word to a different entry is listed, and each change must be intended. Also run it for any older lesson you edited.
9. Open the new pages in the preview (words, lesson, story and quiz tabs; no console errors). Check the stories against the bible below. Update the README roadmap and the bible.

## Writing rules learned so far

- The French columns of a grammar table (`frCols`) are linked word by word and read aloud, so they must contain only French, never English notes.
- A `mistakes` entry must be a real learner error ("je mangera"), never correct French.
- Write *Madame Dupont* in French texts: the abbreviation *Mme* is not in the dictionary (it's fine in speaker names, which aren't linked).
- Tips, rules, mistakes and translations are not linked, so they must not contain `[word|id]` or `{text|case}` markup (`check_app.mjs` flags it).
- Each vocabulary example has one `**target**`, used for fill-in-the-blank questions. The blank hides the target, so the word after it loses its context: *La **reine** porte une couronne* becomes *La _____ porte…*, where *porte* reads as a door. `--homographs` lists these prompts; mark the word: *[porte|porter]*.
- Quiz items with `"lang": "en"` need a French `say` (or `listen`); otherwise the French voice reads the English answer aloud.
- A word card shows the word's own meaning, so use each word in the sense it has in the dictionary: for a machine that works, write *fonctionner*, not *marcher* (= to walk); don't use *personne* for *nobody* (its card says *person*).
- Context doesn't settle a noun/verb homograph after an adjective (*la première partie*: *partie* or *partir*?), or after a noun (*un objet vide*): mark it up, as in *la première [partie|partie]*. A new verb can take an older noun's place this way (*une petite plante* read as *planter*): `--compare-links` lists those texts, so mark them up too. It works the other way round as well: a new verb can take an older adjective's place (*à prix fixe* read as *fixer*: *[fixe|fixe]*), and a new noun a verb's (*le jury leur offre* read as *une offre*: *[offre|offrir]*; *le manger ou le toucher* read as the sense of touch: *le [toucher|toucher]*) or an adjective's after an article (*c'est le marron*, the brown one, read as a chestnut: *le [marron|marron]*). A verb or a preposition doesn't settle it either: write *fait [partie|partie] de*, *de se [souvenir|se-souvenir]*, *par [date|date]*, *sur [commande|commande-n]*; and after an adjective, *son premier [cours|cours]*, *le même [sens|sens-n]*. Nor do *tout le monde* (*tout le monde [danse|danser]*), the infinitive *avoir* (*croire avoir [compris|comprendre]*) or *ce qui* (*ce qui [compte|compter]*); after an adjective, mark *la première [place|place]* and *la petite [partie|partie]*; after a noun, *une boîte [vide|vide]*, *des ombres [dures|dur]*, *à moitié [vide|vide]*; after *en*, *manger en [entrée|entree]* (as a starter, not *entrer*); after *avoir*, *il a [cours|cours] d'espagnol*; after *sans*, *sans [dispute|dispute]*; after a name, *Habitat 67 [compte|compter]*; after *leur*, *ne les leur [montre|montrer] pas*. A number announces a noun, and since Unit 45 so do the plurals *vingts, cents* (*quatre-vingts couches*, not *se coucher*), but *en* doesn't (*partis en [couches|couche]*), and neither does a quiz blank (*Chaque semaine, ___ [couches|couche].*). Outside Unit 45, *dure* goes to the adjective *dur*: *le film [dure|durer] deux heures*.
- A newly taught word wins every tie against a helper with the same spelling, even in older lessons: the new verb *préciser* took the helper adjective in *à une heure précise*, so mark it: *[précise|precis]*. When the new word is the rarer sense (*un prêt*, a loan, against the helper *prêt*, ready), make it `"manual": true` and mark its uses: *un [prêt|pret-n]*. `--compare-links` shows these cases. An interjection spelled like an older word (*mince !*, darn!, next to *mince*, thin) is manual too: *[Mince|mince-interj] !* So is *bien que* (although), because of comparisons (*il chante moins bien que moi*): write *[bien que|bien-que]*. The other conjunctions (*pour que, sans que, avant que…*) link on their own, in older texts too. In its own lesson, a new word wins even against older taught words: where *le toucher* (touch) is taught, *Toucher quelqu'un…* needs *[Toucher|toucher]*. *Faute de* (for lack of) is manual because of *une faute de français*: *[Faute de|faute-de] bûche*. *Vouloir dire* (to mean) isn't: *ça veut dire* links it, so mark *wants to say*: *elle [veut|vouloir] [dire|dire] merci*. *De plus* (what's more), *à savoir* (namely) and *histoire de* (just to) are manual too, because of *un mois de plus*, *commencer à savoir* and *l'histoire de France*: *[De plus|de-plus]*, *[à savoir|a-savoir]*, *[histoire de|histoire-de]*. *La moyenne* (the pass mark) now takes ties after a noun, so mark the adjective there: *une taille [moyenne|moyen]*.
- A country taught in a lesson is a `name` entry with its article (`"art": "la"`) and a `say` with the article (*la Suisse*): said alone, *Suisse* sounds like the adjective *suisse*, and a listening question couldn't tell them apart. A capital picks the name, lower case the adjective (*en Suisse*, *le chocolat suisse*); *un Suisse* would read as the country, so write *les Suisses*.
- A noun with an aspirated h needs `"hAsp": true`, or the word list and the quiz elide its article (*l'hors-jeu*).
- *est* (east) is manual and linked with its article, *à [l'est|est-n]*: a click then plays *l'est* (`ALONE_FIXES`), and `FIXES` say /ɛst/ in *à l'est* and *de l'est*. Avoid *nord-est* and *sud-est*, which read as *être*.
- Entries from the first units can lack forms that later verbs have: *se dépêcher* (u08) had no *dépêcher* until Unit 37. When the validator calls a form of a taught verb unknown, complete the old entry instead of adding a helper (the helper would be a duplicate id).
- Hyphens and spaces match alike: a taught *petit-enfant* (grandchild) would also link *un petit enfant* (a small child), so it is manual, and so is *savoir-faire* (know-how), because of *savoir faire* (to know how to do). Likewise *belle-fille* (daughter-in-law), because of *une belle fille*, and the noun *faire-part* (`faire-part-n`), because of *faire part de* (u40): *le [faire-part|faire-part-n] de naissance*.
- After a noun or a name, an object pronoun is only recognised when the next word can only be a verb (*Inès la rassure*). When that word is also a noun, mark both: *Tom [la|la-pron] [console|consoler]*. For *les* and *l'*, marking the verb is enough: *et les [aide|aider]*, *la thérapie l'[aide|aider]* (otherwise *une aide*).
- Keep the word itself out of its definition: the definition quiz shows the definition, so it would give the answer away (about 200 older definitions still do). Put expressions built on the word in a `note` (*en baisse*, *d'occasion*).
- Every verb now has its past participles (Unit 29) and its present participle (Unit 30). Give a new verb all its forms, *-é, -ée, -és, -ées* and *-ant* included, except those spelled like a taught word, which stay with it: *sécurisé* is not a form of *sécuriser*, nor *méfiant* of *se méfier*, *résistant* (the Resistance fighter) of *résister*, or *employé* of *employer*; and *calme, calmes* belong to the adjective, not to *calmer* (write *calme-toi* or the infinitive). When a new verb covers a helper adjective entirely (*obligé*, from *obliger*; *gêné*, from *gêner*; *interdit*, from *interdire*), delete the helper; a taught adjective keeps its participles (*enfermé*, u29, is not a form of *enfermer*). Don't teach a noun spelled like a grammar word: *un pas* (a step) would take over the negation *pas* everywhere.
- Every word of a vocabulary lesson must appear in its story (`validate.py` checks). A new phrase entry can swallow an older lesson's word (*à mon avis*, *ça dépend*, *c'est ainsi qu'*, taken by *ainsi que*, or u09's *au feu rouge*, taken by *feu rouge* in Unit 46), and an older phrase a new word (*prendre soin des gens* links *prendre soin*, so the new *soin* needs *prendre [soin|soin]*): `validate.py` then names that lesson, and you mark the word back, as in *à mon [avis|avis]* or *au [feu|feu] rouge*.
- Idioms are phrase entries with their conjugated forms (*a la pêche*, *posé un lapin*). A word in the middle (*il a toujours la pêche*, *ne fais pas tout un fromage*) breaks the match, so keep the idiom in one piece, or add the form (*ce n'est pas du gâteau*).
- Give a phrase entry only the forms that can't mean something else: *confiance en soi* has no *confiance en lui* form, which would swallow *avoir confiance en lui* (to trust him).
- The tokenizer reads *avait, était* (and the other imparfait, future and conditional forms of *avoir* and *être*) like *a, est*: the next word is a verb or a participle (*il avait vu, elle était partie, il aurait été, elle serait partie*).
- *été* is read as the participle only right after *avoir* (*elle a été vue*). With a word in between, mark it up: *elle a déjà [été|etre] vue*. The same goes for *passé* after *s'est bien*: *l'oral s'est bien [passé|passer]* (otherwise *le passé*). The word after *été* can also lose to a noun: *ta vidéo a été [vue|voir]*. So can a participle after *pas encore* (*pas encore [arrivée|arriver]*), and an adjective after a comma reads as a verb: *des choses, [prêtes|pret] à vendre* (not *tu prêtes*).
- A taught phrase can swallow the first word of the next one: in *il le tient à l'envers*, *tient à* is read as *tenir à* (to care about), and *à côté du mien* loses *du* to *à côté du*. Reword, or mark the words up. A phrase can also take a word in another sense: *ma mère ne m'appelle plus* was read as *s'appeler* (be called), *le CAP se prépare* as *se préparer* (get ready); mark the verb: *m'[appelle|appeler]*, *se [prépare|preparer]*. The slang *en mode* takes the start of *en mode avion*: *en [mode avion|mode-avion]*. And *c'est ce que* was read as *est-ce que*: write *c'est [ce que|ce-que]*; *ni d'un côté ni de l'autre* (literally) as *d'un côté* (on the one hand): *ni d'un [côté|cote]*. *En gros plan* (in close-up) reads as *en gros* (basically): *en [gros plan|gros-plan]*; *tu as mal cadré* as *avoir mal*: *tu as [mal|mal] cadré*.
- Inversion with *-t-* splits into three words (*a, t, il*), so each form is a phrase helper (`pos: expr`): *a-t-il, va-t-on, pense-t-elle, y a-t-il*… Add one for any new form.
- A reflexive verb and its plain verb share their bare forms, and the older entry wins (a taught word beats a helper). The pronoun forms (*se retrouve, me cache*) are phrases and keep the reflexive sense, so a reflexive helper needs them (`@er-refl`): when Unit 45 taught *retrouver* and *cacher*, the helpers *se retrouver* and *se cacher* got them. The passé composé isn't covered: *ils se sont [retrouvés|se-retrouver]*. *Se rendre* (to go) is manual, because its forms are also *rendre* with a pronoun (*tu peux me rendre mon livre*) and *se rendre compte*: *je dois [me rendre|se-rendre] à la boulangerie*.
- Spoken forms are phrase entries. *T'as* links on its own, but *t'es* (*tu t'es levé*) and *y a* (*elle y a pensé*) are manual: *[T'es|t-es] où ?*, *[Y a|y-a] du monde*, *[y avait|y-a]*. So are fillers spelled like ordinary words (*[tu vois|tu-vois], [tu sais|tu-sais], [je veux dire|je-veux-dire], [tu parles|tu-parles], [À plus|a-plus] !*) the verlan *[ouf|ouf-verlan]* (*ouf !* is *phew!*), and *[puis-je|puis-je]* (*puis je* = then I). *T'inquiète* ties with a form of *s'inquiéter*: older texts keep the verb, its own lesson gets the expression, and elsewhere you mark it: *[t'inquiète|t-inquiete]*.
- Punctuation hides the context: in *Tom, lui, court*, the comma makes *court* read as *short*, so mark it: *[court|courir]*. Participles that are also adjectives or nouns need the same care: *j'ai rien [compris|comprendre]*, *il a tout [compris|comprendre]* (otherwise *tout compris*, all included), *étant [arrivée|arriver]* (not *une arrivée*), and the other way round, *être [reconnaissant|reconnaissant]* (grateful), read as *reconnaître*. A list of verbs hides the context too: *on chante, joue ou [danse|danser]*.
- The stories share one recurring cast (below). Keep ages, places and past events consistent from unit to unit.
